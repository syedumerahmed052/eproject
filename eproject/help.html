🏅 About the Prize

Nobel Prize Questions

The Nobel Prize is a set of annual international awards bestowed in several categories by Swedish and Norwegian institutions. Established by the will of Alfred Nobel in 1895, the prizes have been awarded since 1901 in Physics, Chemistry, Physiology or Medicine, Literature, and Peace. A sixth prize — in Economic Sciences — was added in 1968.
Alfred Bernhard Nobel (1833–1896) was a Swedish chemist, engineer, and inventor best known for inventing dynamite. Troubled by the destructive use of his inventions, he left the bulk of his considerable fortune to establish the Nobel Prizes, hoping to fund work that benefits humanity.
There are six categories: Physics, Chemistry, Physiology or Medicine, Literature, Peace, and Economic Sciences (officially the Sveriges Riksbank Prize in Economic Sciences in Memory of Alfred Nobel, added in 1968).
Each laureate receives a Nobel Medal (18-karat gold), a Nobel Diploma (a unique work of art), and a cash award. The prize money has varied over the years; as of recent years it stands at 11 million Swedish kronor (approximately $1 million USD) per prize category. If a prize is shared, the sum is divided accordingly.
Since 1974, the Nobel Prize may not be awarded posthumously. However, if a laureate dies after the announcement but before the award ceremony (held on 10 December, the anniversary of Alfred Nobel's death), the prize is still conferred.

🇵🇰 Pakistan & Nobel

Pakistan's Laureates

Two. Dr. Abdus Salam won the Nobel Prize in Physics in 1979, and Malala Yousafzai won the Nobel Peace Prize in 2014.
Dr. Abdus Salam was the first Pakistani to win a Nobel Prize. His theoretical work on the electroweak unification — showing that two of the four fundamental forces of nature are manifestations of a single force — remains one of the most important discoveries in modern physics. He also founded the ICTP in Trieste, which has trained generations of scientists from developing nations.
Malala Yousafzai was 17 years old when she was awarded the Nobel Peace Prize on 10 December 2014, making her the youngest recipient in Nobel Prize history. She was recognised for her struggle against the suppression of children and young people and for the right of all children to education.
Yes. The 2014 Nobel Peace Prize was shared between Malala Yousafzai and Kailash Satyarthi of India. The Norwegian Nobel Committee highlighted that a Muslim and a Hindu, a Pakistani and an Indian, were jointly awarded to send a message of solidarity and fraternity between two nations.
