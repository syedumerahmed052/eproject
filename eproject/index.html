Nobel Laureates from Pakistan

Two remarkable figures who changed the world — and made Pakistan proud on the global stage.

Physics · 1979

Dr. Abdus Salam

Nobel Prize in Physics
"Scientific thought is the common heritage of mankind."

Pakistan's first Nobel Laureate, honoured for his contributions to the electroweak unification theory, one of the cornerstones of modern particle physics. He co-founded ICTP in Trieste, dedicating his life to science in the developing world.

Peace · 2014

Malala Yousafzai

Nobel Peace Prize
"One child, one teacher, one book, one pen can change the world."

The youngest Nobel Prize winner in history. Born in the Swat Valley, she defied the Taliban's ban on girls' education and survived an assassination attempt to become the world's most prominent advocate for every child's right to learn.

A Legacy in Time

Pakistan's Nobel Journey

1979

Dr. Abdus Salam wins Physics Nobel

Awarded for theoretical work on the unified weak and electromagnetic interaction — the first Pakistani to win a Nobel Prize.

1984

ICTP named for Abdus Salam

The International Centre for Theoretical Physics in Trieste, which he founded in 1964, is officially renamed in his honour.

2012

Malala survives assassination attempt

Shot by Taliban gunmen in Mingora, Swat — her survival and recovery galvanised global support for girls' education.

2014

Malala receives Nobel Peace Prize

At age 17, Malala Yousafzai becomes the youngest Nobel laureate in history, sharing the prize with India's Kailash Satyarthi.

🌍 Around the World

International Nobel Laureates

A selection of the world's most celebrated Nobel Prize winners whose work changed humanity.

Physics

Albert Einstein

1921

Awarded for his discovery of the law of the photoelectric effect, foundational to quantum theory. His theory of relativity reshaped our understanding of space, time, and gravity.

Physics & Chemistry

Marie Curie

1903 & 1911

The only person to win Nobel Prizes in two different sciences. Her pioneering research on radioactivity opened an entirely new field of scientific inquiry.

Peace

Martin Luther King Jr.

1964

Recognised for leading the non-violent civil rights movement in the United States, fighting racial discrimination and injustice with courage and dignity.

Peace

Nelson Mandela

1993

Honoured for his lifelong struggle against apartheid and his role in South Africa's peaceful democratic transition after 27 years of imprisonment.

Peace

Mother Teresa

1979

Awarded for her decades of work among the poorest of the poor in Calcutta — founding the Missionaries of Charity and serving humanity with extraordinary compassion.

Literature

Rabindranath Tagore

1913

The first non-European Nobel Laureate in Literature, honoured for his profoundly sensitive, fresh, and beautiful verse from his collection Gitanjali.
